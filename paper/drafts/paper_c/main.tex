       Six Failure Modes and a Methodology for Honest Evaluation of Augmented LLMs}

\begin{abstract}
We began with a simple question: why did a recurrent memory module
sometimes look useful, sometimes invisible, and sometimes actively
harmful on the same long-conversation callback task?  In a
$16$-version campaign on Memory Residuals --- a slot-attention memory
matrix attached to Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B --- the answer was not a
single bug.  It was an evaluation problem.  Token-averaged chain
evaluation diluted a localized callback gain by roughly two orders of
magnitude; frozen and joint training measured different causal
pathways; the synthetic D4v2 callback template leaked a $32$-way
category prior that joint-trained models matched directly; more than
half of the D4v2 callback windows exposed evidence to the LM path;
evidence redaction had to be audited before a zero lift could be
trusted; and a random-ID corpus removed the prior but exposed a
writer objective that still did not force chain-specific bindings into
$\Mc$.  We turn these failures into a six-item methodology:
callback-aware evaluation, evidence-redacted memory floors, empirical
and base-model prior audits, window-leakage counts, a test-time
readout audit that asks whether the writer encoded the answer at all,
and a held-out-corpus pivot that breaks the templated-data confound.
Applying the methodology end-to-end yields a single positive recipe
--- a frozen pretrained backbone with a fixed-size jointly-trained
$\Mc$, supervised by LM-NLL alone --- that delivers
$+1.32 \pm 0.53$~nats of chain-specific callback CE improvement on
LongMemEval-S validation across four seeds at Qwen3-0.6B, with a
chain-shuffle confound statistically zero.  The methodology rejects
all of our earlier published-looking results.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A practical conversational agent should remember a user across
sessions.  Three architectural routes for that capability dominate
the contemporary literature: \emph{joint training}, in which a memory
module and the language-model backbone are trained together
end-to-end; \emph{frozen backbone}, in which a small recurrent state
is added to a pretrained model whose parameters are then held fixed;
and \emph{retrieval}, in which past sessions are indexed and re-fed
through the attention window at query time.  Papers in each of the
three programs report success.  Papers from the three programs
rarely fail in the same way.  And papers from the three programs
rarely report the same evaluation controls.

This paper contains a finding that does not look like a finding when
described in any one of the three vocabularies.  In a $16$-version
post-mortem of \emph{Memory Residuals} --- a slot-attention recurrent
memory module attached to Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B that we are able
to train under all three regimes on the same architecture --- we
encountered six independent failure modes.  Each is sufficient by
itself to make a published-style result look positive when the result
is not actually carrying chain-specific information.  Five of the six
have been observed individually, in one or another of the three
research programs, but never together; the sixth (writer-objective
failure) is, to our knowledge, novel as a documented memory-side
pathology.  The failure modes are independent: fixing one does not
fix the others.

The diagnostic state of the literature is that each program checks
roughly the failure modes the others do not.  A joint-training paper
typically reports task accuracy and a no-memory baseline, which dodges
token-averaging dilution but leaves the joint-training-invisibility
mode and the template-prior leak unmeasured.  A frozen-backbone paper
typically reports a chain-CE delta, which exposes the
joint-invisibility mode by construction but masks token-averaging
dilution and rarely reports an evidence-redacted floor.  A retrieval
paper typically reports a recall@$k$ and a downstream-QA accuracy,
which catches some surface-form leakage but does not address the
chain-shuffle confound.  Cross-program, no single paper to our
knowledge reports all six controls; most report two or fewer
(Table~\ref{tab:ideologies}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}  This paper makes three contributions and
one empirical surprise.

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.5pt}
\item \textbf{A field-level diagnosis.}  Three research programs of
recurrent memory (joint training, frozen backbone, retrieval) cluster
into three evaluation conventions whose reporting matrices do not
overlap.  We describe and tabulate the gap (\S\ref{sec:ideologies}).
\item \textbf{Six failure modes (\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}).}  We
catalogue six independent ways a recurrent-memory result can look
positive without carrying chain-specific information; each is
load-bearing on at least one ideology, each is detected by a single
cheap diagnostic, and we exhibit a worked numerical example from the
campaign for each.
\item \textbf{Six-item audit methodology (\S\ref{sec:methodology}).}
Callback-aware evaluation, evidence-redacted memory floors, empirical
and base-model prior audits, window-leakage counts, a test-time
readout audit, and a held-out-corpus pivot.  Total compute cost
$<\!1$~H100-hour per checkpoint, applies to all three ideologies.
\item \textbf{An empirical surprise (\S\ref{sec:f3_off}).}  The
auxiliary readout-probe loss that the joint-training literature
canonically prescribes for the writer-objective failure mode is, in
our setting, \emph{harmful}: removing it multiplies $\dnm$ by
$\sim\!8\times$ at both 0.6B and 1.7B, with a chain-level paired
permutation test giving $p < 10^{-4}$.  The recipe that survives all
six audits is the simpler, probe-free one
(\S\ref{sec:recipe_survived}).
\end{enumerate}

\S\ref{sec:limitations} lays out which of the six failure modes
generalize by construction (those that live in the metric or the
corpus, not the architecture) and which we observe only in our
specific slot-attention recipe at $\le 1.7$B.

\section{Related Work and Three Ideologies of Recurrent Memory}
\label{sec:ideologies}

We use the word \emph{ideology} in the modest sense of a research
program with its own characteristic evaluation conventions; nothing
in this paper rests on a stronger philosophical reading.  The three
programs are not interchangeable engineering choices.  Each makes a
different bet about where the chain-specific binding lives, each is
exposed to a different subset of the failure modes catalogued in
\S\ref{sec:failure_modes}, and each tends to report a different
subset of controls.

\paragraph{Joint training.}  The bet is that gradient descent over
the entire model will allocate credit to whichever path carries the
chain-specific binding.  Examples: AutoCompressors~\cite{autocompressors2023},
Memorising Transformers~\cite{memorizing2022}, TRIME~\cite{trime2022},
RIMs~\cite{rims2021}, and the differentiable-memory family
(NTM/DNC~\cite{ntm2014}).  This program is exposed by construction to
\emph{joint-training invisibility} (FM2, \S\ref{sec:fm2}) and to
\emph{template-prior leak} (FM3, \S\ref{sec:fm3}): when there is more
than one path through which the LM head can solve the callback,
gradient descent allocates credit to the easier one, and the memory
channel can collapse to noise.

\paragraph{Frozen backbone.}  The bet is that freezing the LM head
removes the non-memory paths by construction; if memory helps, the
help has to come through $\Mc$.  Examples: the Recurrent Memory
Transformer in its frozen-LM ablation~\cite{rmt2022,armt2024}, the
Block-Recurrent Transformer~\cite{blockrecurrent2022}, and the
Compressive Transformer~\cite{compressive2019}.  The Memory Residuals
substrate we use falls in this program.  This program escapes FM2/FM3
but is heavily exposed to \emph{token-averaged dilution} (FM1,
\S\ref{sec:fm1}) --- a frozen backbone's CE delta is small relative
to the score window --- and to \emph{redaction uncertainty} (FM5,
\S\ref{sec:fm5}): a zero evidence-lift could mean either a
content-blind writer or a leaky redaction definition.  Without an
audit, the two cannot be distinguished.

\paragraph{Retrieval.}  The bet is that recurrent state is
unnecessary: index past sessions, retrieve at query time, append to
context.  Examples: RAG~\cite{ragoriginal2020}, the kNN variant of
Memorising Transformers~\cite{memorizing2022}, and
DOS-RAG~\cite{ragbaseline2025}.  This program is exposed to
\emph{window leakage} (FM4, \S\ref{sec:fm4}) by construction, since
retrieved evidence enters the LM attention window deliberately, and
to surface-form/literal-match shortcuts that the
long-context-evaluation literature
(NoLiMa~\cite{nolima2025}, RULER~\cite{ruler2024},
Lost-in-the-Middle~\cite{lostmiddle2024}) has documented for
retrieval but rarely connected back to memory architectures.

\paragraph{The long-context evaluation cluster.}  The closest
neighbour to this paper's methodology is the long-context-evaluation
literature.  NoLiMa~\cite{nolima2025} removes literal overlap between
query and evidence; RULER~\cite{ruler2024} adds UUIDs and hard
distractors; Lost-in-the-Middle~\cite{lostmiddle2024} documents
position effects; LongBench~\cite{longbench2024} introduces
no-context baselines; LongMemEval~\cite{longmemeval2025} separates
indexing, retrieval, and reading; BABILong~\cite{babilong2024} embeds
bAbI facts in PG19 distractors; ZeroSCROLLS~\cite{zeroscrolls2023}
contributes contamination-controlled zero-shot evaluation.
Geirhos et al.~\cite{shortcut2020} provide the broader frame:
shortcut learning is the default when the benchmark permits it.  This
cluster has imported anti-shortcut techniques from the
benchmark-design literature but has not yet exported them back into
recurrent-memory architecture evaluation; one contribution of this
paper is precisely that re-export.

The pattern in Table~\ref{tab:ideologies} is the field-level finding.
None of the three programs is self-validating.  An honest evaluation
of recurrent memory has to rule out the failure modes its own
ideology is blind to and the failure modes the other two ideologies
are sensitive to.  The contribution of this paper is to consolidate
the controls into a single battery that survives across all three.

\section{Memory Residuals: A Cross-Regime Substrate}
\label{sec:memres}

We use Memory Residuals as a substrate, not as a proposed method.
It is, to our knowledge, the only recurrent-memory architecture
that admits all three ideological framings on the same parameter
count and same corpus, which is what makes the cross-regime
comparison in this paper possible.

\paragraph{State.}  A fixed-size memory $\Mc \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times d}$
with $K=128$ slots and $d$ the backbone hidden size.  $\Mc$ is updated
once per session via a two-stage Extract+Judge slot-attention writer
and read back into the LM through a depth-routed cross-attention
residual.

\paragraph{Extract.}  Given a session token-context representation
$\Ct \in \mathbb{R}^{N_t \times d}$ from a fixed backbone layer
(\texttt{hidden\_14} with input RMSNorm in our setup), a learned
query bank $\Min$ cross-attends to produce a candidate memory
contribution:
\begin{equation}
\Mnew = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\Min W_Q^{\mathrm{ext}}(\Ct W_K^{\mathrm{ext}})^\top}{\sqrt d}\right)\Ct W_V^{\mathrm{ext}}.
\label{eq:extract}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Judge.}  A slot-attention writer with $3$ iterations,
orthogonal query initialisation, slot positional embeddings, and
judge QK layer-norm combines the previous state $\Mc^{t-1}$ with the
candidate $\Mnew$:
\begin{equation}
\Mc^{t} = \mathrm{Slot}_{\theta}\!\left([\Mc^{t-1} \,\|\, \Mnew]\right).
\label{eq:judge}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Read.}  At inference, token representations $X$ at each
routed sublayer cross-attend to $\Mc$ to produce a memory readout
$\mt$:
\begin{equation}
\mt = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{X W_Q^{\mathrm{r}}(\Mc W_K^{\mathrm{r}})^\top}{\sqrt d}\right)\Mc W_V^{\mathrm{r}}.
\label{eq:read}
\end{equation}
The readout $\mt$ is registered as an additional source in a
Block-Attention-Residual router; a learned routing mass
$\amem \in [0,1]$ controls the share of LM computation that uses
memory at each routed sublayer.  Two architectural details turn out
to be load-bearing in the surviving recipe (\S\ref{sec:recipe_survived}):
an iterative readout depth (re-applying the readout cross-attention
$L_R{=}4$ times before residual injection) and a learned $\amem$-floor
that prevents $\amem \to 0$ collapse during training.

\paragraph{Three regimes on the same architecture.}  The same code
path supports three ideological regimes by changing only the
\texttt{--freeze\_backbone}, \texttt{--readout\_probe\_loss\_weight},
and \texttt{--retrieval\_index} flags.  All six failure modes
catalogued in \S\ref{sec:failure_modes} are exhibited on this single
architecture under the appropriate regime, which is what allows the
cross-regime comparison without confounding architecture and ideology.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Architecture.}  All experiments use Memory Residuals
(\S\ref{sec:memres}) attached to two frozen pretrained backbones:
Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B.  Only the slot-attention writer and the
depth-routed readout are trained; the LM head and all backbone
parameters are held fixed throughout.  Trainable parameter count:
$41.5$M at 0.6B ($\sim\!6\%$ overhead) and $164.8$M at 1.7B
($\sim\!8\%$).  Full hyperparameters are reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:arch}.

\paragraph{Corpora.}  The campaign uses three corpora at different
stages.  The \emph{synthetic persona corpus} (campaign label D4v2)
consists of multi-session chats where each chain establishes a
closed-set persona attribute (e.g.\ a favourite colour drawn from
$32$ categories) in an evidence session and queries it via a
templated callback (``What was my favorite \textsc{color}?'').  It
is the corpus most exposed to the template-prior leak (FM3) and to
window leakage at small \texttt{window\_k} (FM4).  The
\emph{random-code corpus} (D5) replaces closed-set attributes with
per-chain random alphanumeric codes and masks the LM loss on
evidence sessions, leaving the callback span as the only supervised
answer location; this corpus removes the template prior and is the
diagnostic for the writer-objective failure (FM6).
\emph{LongMemEval-S}~\cite{longmemeval2025} is the natural-text
held-out evaluation corpus; we evaluate on the validation split,
$50$ chains.

\paragraph{Metrics.}  For a chain $c$ scored at its callback
position, let $\CEmem(c)$ denote the cross-entropy on callback
tokens under the trained model with $\Mc$ built from the chain's
prefix sessions; $\CEnomem(c)$ under $\Mc$ zeroed; $\CEsh(c)$ under
$\Mc$ built from a different chain (the chain-shuffle confound
control); $\CEfloor(c)$ under $\Mc$ built from a chain whose
evidence sessions have been replaced by distractor sessions sampled
from filler positions of unrelated chains.  We define
\begin{align*}
\dnm     &= \CEnomem - \CEmem,                       &&\text{chain-specific memory benefit;} \\
\dsh     &= \CEsh    - \CEmem,                       &&\text{history-specificity confound;} \\
\elift   &= \dnm     - (\CEnomem - \CEfloor),        &&\text{evidence-specific lift over the no-evidence floor.}
\end{align*}
Standard chain-CE evaluation aggregates across all tokens of the
scored window.  \emph{Callback-aware} evaluation, denoted
$\dnm^{\mathrm{cb}}$, restricts the score to the annotated callback
answer tokens only.

\paragraph{Recipes and naming convention.}  We refer to two recipe
families that differ in a single training flag.  \textsc{F3-on}
adds an auxiliary readout-side probe loss that asks the readout to
predict a chain-identifying signal directly from $\Mc$.
\textsc{F3-off} disables this auxiliary loss; only the long-path
LM-NLL gradient supervises the writer and readout.  In the campaign
ledger, \textsc{F3-on} cells are labelled v24a (0.6B) and v25a
(1.7B); \textsc{F3-off} cells are v27b (0.6B) and v28 (1.7B).  All
other architectural and optimisation details match across the two
recipes; the only flag that varies is
\texttt{--readout\_probe\_loss\_weight}.

\paragraph{Compute and seeds.}  Each 0.6B run consumes approximately
$1.5$ H100-hours; each 1.7B run approximately $6$ H100-hours.  The
headline F3-OFF flip is established at $n=4$ seeds at 0.6B and
$n=2$ seeds at 1.7B, with their F3-ON counterparts at $n=3$ and
$n=2$ respectively.  Total campaign compute, including unreported
preliminary runs from earlier ablations, is approximately $400$
H100-hours on H100 80GB hardware.

\paragraph{Statistical testing.}  Headline numerical claims are
accompanied by bootstrap $95\%$ confidence intervals over the $50$
LongMemEval-S validation chains.  The F3-OFF flip is certified by a
chain-level paired sign-flip permutation test ($10{,}000$
permutations, one-sided) and a paired sign-test, both reported in
\S\ref{sec:f3_off} and detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:perm}.  We
apply a Bonferroni correction across the six failure-mode claims to
control the family-wise error rate.

\section{Six Failure Modes}
\label{sec:failure_modes}

An apparent recurrent-memory result has to be ruled out from five
non-memory pathways: a \emph{content-blind memory prior} (the writer
emits a similar $\Mc$ on every chain and the readout shifts the LM
toward a marginal callback distribution); a \emph{backbone template
prior} (an unfrozen LM head learns the closed-set callback
distribution directly from training data with $\Mc$ unused); a
\emph{window path} (evidence remains in the local LM attention
window at scoring time); and two \emph{measurement pathways}
(token-averaging dilutes a localised callback gain, and an unaudited
redaction definition makes a zero-lift floor look leaky or vice
versa).  Each pathway corresponds to one or two of the failure modes
catalogued below; the mapping is summarised in
Table~\ref{tab:fm_mapping}.

The six failures are independent: fixing one does not fix the
others, which we verify in \S\ref{sec:methodology} via the
audit-battery composition argument.  Numerical anchors throughout
this section come from F3-ON checkpoints unless otherwise stated.

\subsection{FM1.\enspace Token-averaged dilution}
\label{sec:fm1}

Chain-level cross-entropy averages over the entire scoring window,
but chain-specific information is concentrated in the
\emph{callback answer tokens} that comprise only $1$--$2\%$ of a
window.  A frozen-backbone F3-ON checkpoint at an early-campaign
snapshot improves callback-aware CE by $\dnm^{\mathrm{cb}} = +1.44$~nats
while \emph{worsening} chain-level CE by $\dnm = -0.085$~nats: the
noise on the remaining $98\%$ of tokens dominates the mean.  The
diagnostic is to score CE only on the annotated answer mask
(\emph{callback-aware CE}, \texttt{tools/eval\_callback.py}).  This
failure is most acute in the frozen-backbone program, where
chain-level CE is the conventional headline metric; joint-training
and retrieval papers escape it by reporting task accuracy, but they
pay for that escape with their own failure modes (FM2, FM3).

\subsection{FM2.\enspace Joint-training invisibility}
\label{sec:fm2}

A joint-trained backbone collapses $\elift$ to approximately zero
even on corpora where the frozen-backbone checkpoint shows positive
callback gain.  Joint training opens a non-memory path through the
LM head; gradient descent allocates credit to whichever path is
shortest, and the LM-head path is shorter than the
writer$\to\Mc\to$readout$\to$LM-head path.  On the synthetic persona
corpus, a joint-trained 0.6B run reaches callback CE within
$0.06$~nats of the empirical $32$-way category prior ($2.91$~nats
per callback-mask token), and $\elift \approx 0$: the LM head learned
the prior, $\Mc$ is unused.  The diagnostic is to compare
frozen-backbone and joint-training versions of the same architecture
at matched data, and to report the empirical prior CE explicitly.
If joint matches the prior within $0.1$~nats and frozen does not,
the joint result is ``the LM learned the prior'', not
``the memory learned the binding''.

\subsection{FM3.\enspace Category-cue leak / template prior}
\label{sec:fm3}

Synthetic callback corpora that repeat a closed-set category in the
user question (``What was my favorite \textsc{color} again?'')
collapse the answer distribution to a sharp, easily-fit prior.  The
empirical $P(\text{item} \mid \text{category})$ on the synthetic
persona corpus has CE $2.9087$~nats per callback-mask token, so any
joint-trained baseline within $\sim\!0.1$~nats of $2.91$ is
consistent with ``LM head fits the prior'' with no memory
contribution required.  The diagnostic is a Laplace-smoothed
empirical prior reported explicitly as a baseline
(\texttt{tools/audit\_a3\_template\_prior.py}).  Joint-training
papers are most exposed; retrieval is partially exposed because the
LM head still fits the prior even with retrieved context;
frozen-backbone is immune by construction \emph{provided} the LM
head is truly frozen --- a non-trivial provision because some
adapter-style fine-tuning leaks unfrozen capacity.

\subsection{FM4.\enspace Window leakage}
\label{sec:fm4}

Evidence sessions can remain inside the LM attention window at
callback scoring time even when a corpus declares them ``out of
window''.  On the synthetic persona corpus at the default
\texttt{window\_k}{=}4, $58.5\%$ of training chains and $59.6\%$ of
validation chains have at least one evidence session inside the
callback scoring window: memory may appear to help only because
evidence is in-window.  The diagnostic is to count the per-chain
leak fraction (\texttt{tools/audit\_a1\_window\_leakage.py}) and
report it for the chosen \texttt{window\_k}.  Retrieval relies on
this path deliberately, so retrieval papers should disclose the leak
count rather than wishing it away; frozen and joint backbones are
exposed unless the corpus enforces a strict ``evidence outside
window'' gap.

\subsection{FM5.\enspace Redaction uncertainty}
\label{sec:fm5}

The frozen-backbone evidence-redacted floor underpins the
$\elift = \dnm - (\CEnomem - \CEfloor)$ measurement.  But
$\elift \approx 0$ admits two interpretations: (a) the writer is not
encoding chain-specific information (real null), or (b) the
redaction is leaky and $\Mc$ built from ``redacted'' data still
contains the answer (false null).  The diagnostic is to compare four
redaction variants (default, cross-chain, skip-evidence,
zeroed-token) and require an inter-variant spread $\le 0.05$~nats
before trusting an $\elift \approx 0$ reading
(\texttt{tools/audit\_a2\_redaction.py}).  On a representative F3-ON
0.6B checkpoint the spread is $0.038$~nats and the default floor is
slightly worse than the cross/zero variants, so the floor is not
leaking in the dangerous direction.  The diagnostic costs $4\times$
a single $\dnm$ evaluation.

\subsection{FM6.\enspace Writer-objective failure}
\label{sec:fm6}

A random-code corpus (per-chain alphanumeric ID, evidence-session
LM loss masked, no category cue) removes the template prior of FM3
and the partial leak of FM4 --- and yet $\elift$ remains near zero.
An F3-ON 0.6B run on the random-code corpus records
$\elift = -0.0024$~nats early in training and at most $+0.011$ at
later checkpoints, with $\dnm^{\mathrm{cb}} = -0.076$ and
$\amem \approx 0.01$.  The cause is not data; it is a writer-side
optimisation problem.  The writer receives gradient only through the
long path callback NLL$\to$LM head$\to$depth router$\to$readout$\to$
$\Mc\to$writer; without a category prior, ``wrong code'' and
``uninformative memory'' both look bad to the LM loss, so there is
no direct extractive signal.  The diagnostic is a \emph{test-time
readout audit}: freeze all parameters except the readout's
$W_Q,W_K,W_V$ and TTT-optimise on callback tokens of held-out
chains.  If the resulting CE drops below the floor by more than
$0.30$~nats, the writer encoded the answer and the readout was the
bottleneck; if not, the writer never encoded the literal answer
(\texttt{tools/eval\_ttt\_mc\_readout.py}).  Frozen-backbone is most
exposed; any architecture whose memory module is trained only by
long-path gradient through the LM head is at risk.  Retrieval is
exempt because it has a separate index-time objective.

\section{Headline Result: The Auxiliary Readout Probe Backfires}
\label{sec:f3_off}

The natural fix for the writer-objective failure (FM6) is the
auxiliary readout-side probe loss --- the F3 probe of
\S\ref{sec:setup} --- that asks the readout to predict a
chain-identifying signal directly from $\Mc$.  F3-ON yields modest
gains: $\dnm = +0.16 \pm 0.08$~nats at 0.6B ($n=3$ seeds) and
$+0.12$~nats at 1.7B ($n=2$ seeds).  Switching the F3 probe off ---
the only flag change being the auxiliary-loss weight ---
multiplies $\dnm$ by $\sim\!8\times$ at both scales
(Table~\ref{tab:f3_off}): $+1.32 \pm 0.53$~nats at 0.6B ($n=4$ seeds)
and $+0.93$~nats at 1.7B ($n=2$ seeds).  Two single-variable
ablations (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}) confirm load-bearing
components of the surviving recipe: removing the iterative readout
depth collapses $\dnm$ to $+0.025$, and removing the $\amem$-floor
drives it to $-0.04$.  Removing the F3 probe alone, by contrast,
\emph{strengthens} the result.

\paragraph{Chain-level paired permutation test.}
The seed-level standard deviation at $n=4$ ($0.530$~nats) is large
relative to the mean.  We therefore certify the effect against a
chain-level paired sign-flip permutation test that exploits the $50$
paired chains rather than the $7$ paired seeds.  For each chain $c$
define
\begin{equation}
\delta_c
  = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{s \in \mathrm{F3\text{-}OFF}}
        \!\bigl[\CEnomem^{(s)}(c) - \CEmem^{(s)}(c)\bigr]
  - \frac{1}{3}\sum_{s \in \mathrm{F3\text{-}ON}}
        \!\bigl[\CEnomem^{(s)}(c) - \CEmem^{(s)}(c)\bigr].
\end{equation}
Under $H_0$ (F3 has no causal effect on chain-level memory benefit),
F3-ON/F3-OFF labels are exchangeable per chain, so
$\mathrm{sign}(\delta_c)$ is i.i.d.\ $\pm 1$.  The observed mean is
$+1.16$~nats per chain (95\% bootstrap CI $[+0.84, +1.52]$); zero
of $10{,}000$ sign-flip permutations matched its magnitude
(one-sided $p < 10^{-4}$, Figure~\ref{fig:permtest} left).  The
effect is not driven by a few chains: $47$ of $50$ chains favour
F3-OFF (one-sided sign-test $p = 1.9\times10^{-11}$), the per-chain
median is $+0.60$~nats, and the worst chain is within $0.07$~nats of
zero (Figure~\ref{fig:permtest} right).  Each of the twelve
cross-seed pairwise comparisons has F3-OFF winning on at least
$42$ of $50$ chains (range $42$--$49$); the 1.7B replication records
$44/50$ chains positive at each individual seed.

\paragraph{Why the F3 probe hurts.}
The F3 probe asks the readout to recover a chain-identifying signal
directly from $\Mc$.  Joint LM-NLL training alone produces a richer
chain-conditional context than supervising the readout for chain
identification: the probe's gradient pulls the readout toward a
chain-ID decoder, while the LM-NLL gradient asks the readout to
compress the chain into a context vector that lowers next-token loss
on \emph{any} future callback in the chain.  Removing the probe lets
the latter dominate.  $\dsh \approx 0$ across all six F3-OFF seeds
confirms the resulting $\Mc$ is chain-specific; the small $\elift$
confirms that the writer is compressing chain-conditional context
(style, topic, vocabulary, prior turns) rather than memorising the
literal answer-bearing session.  This makes the F3 prescription a
falsified ideology-level intervention: auxiliary readout supervision
is the standard fix proposed in the joint-training literature for
FM6 and is empirically shown here to backfire.

\section{Six-Item Audit Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

The audit battery is the constructive output of the campaign: a
single set of controls that any recurrent-memory paper can run in
under one H100-hour per checkpoint, regardless of ideology.

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.5pt}
\item \textbf{Callback-aware CE} (FM1).  Restrict scoring to
annotated callback answer tokens.  Released as
\texttt{tools/eval\_callback.py}.
\item \textbf{Evidence-redacted memory floor} (FM5, FM6).  Build
$\Mc$ from a chain whose evidence sessions are replaced by
distractor sessions sampled from filler positions of other chains;
the resulting $\dnm$ is the no-evidence floor that
$\elift = \dnm - (\CEnomem - \CEfloor)$ measures against.  The floor
itself is validated by comparing default, cross-chain,
skip-evidence, and zeroed-token redaction variants; require
$\le 0.05$~nat spread between variants before trusting an
$\elift \approx 0$ reading
(\texttt{tools/audit\_a2\_redaction.py}).
\item \textbf{Empirical and base-model prior audits} (FM2, FM3).
Build a Laplace-smoothed $P(\text{item}\mid\text{category})$ prior
over the corpus and a base-model callback CE without memory or
fine-tuning.  Memory results that do not beat both priors are not
memory results.  Released as
\texttt{tools/audit\_a3\_template\_prior.py} and
\texttt{tools/audit\_base\_prior.py}.
\item \textbf{Window-leakage count} (FM4).  Count the fraction of
chains where the gold evidence session falls inside the callback
scoring window at the chosen \texttt{window\_k}.  Released as
\texttt{tools/audit\_a1\_window\_leakage.py}.
\item \textbf{Test-time readout audit} (FM6).  TTT only the readout
$W_Q,W_K,W_V$ on callback tokens of held-out chains; if CE drops
below the floor by more than $0.30$~nats, the writer encoded the
answer and the readout was the bottleneck; if not, the writer is
the bottleneck.  Released as
\texttt{tools/eval\_ttt\_mc\_readout.py}.  Numerical results on the
surviving recipe are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:ttt_readout};
all $7$ checkpoints under all $3$ TTT modes return ``writer is the
bottleneck'', supporting the chain-conditional-context
interpretation.
\item \textbf{Held-out-corpus pivot.}  Report results on a corpus
that the architecture has not been corpus-tuned against and that
differs qualitatively from the training corpus (e.g.\ a synthetic
closed-set persona corpus $\to$ a natural multi-session dialogue
corpus).  In our campaign, results that looked positive on the
synthetic persona corpus for the wrong reasons (FM3 + FM4)
collapsed to honest numbers on LongMemEval-S without recipe changes.
This is the cross-corpus analogue of the per-checkpoint audits and
is necessary for ruling out template-corpus confounds that no single
per-checkpoint diagnostic catches.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why the six are independent.}  Each audit closes a
distinct non-memory pathway (Table~\ref{tab:fm_mapping}); fixing one
pathway does not block the others.  Token-averaging (FM1) lives in
the metric, the joint-training prior (FM2/FM3) in the architecture
choice, the window leak (FM4) in the corpus, the redaction
ambiguity (FM5) in the floor definition, the writer collapse (FM6)
in the optimisation regime, and the held-out-corpus pivot in the
cross-corpus experiment design.  Six different surfaces; six
different diagnostics.

\section{The Recipe That Survived All Six Audits}
\label{sec:recipe_survived}

Applying the audit battery prospectively across the campaign,
exactly one configuration survives all six audits with positive
chain-specific evidence: a frozen Qwen3 backbone, a fixed-size
slot-attention $\Mc$ with $K=128$, depth-routed readout with
iterative readout depth $L_R{=}4$, an $\amem$-floor on the depth
router, and \emph{no} auxiliary writer or readout probe.  In
campaign labels this is v27b at 0.6B and v28 at 1.7B; the ablation
table (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}) shows the load-bearing components
are the readout depth and the $\amem$-floor; the canonically
prescribed F3 readout probe is, as \S\ref{sec:f3_off} reports,
harmful.

\paragraph{Headline survival numbers.}  Memory-on vs.\ memory-off
callback CE on $50$ LongMemEval-S validation chains under the
surviving recipe: $\dnm = +1.32 \pm 0.53$~nats at 0.6B ($n=4$
seeds), $\dnm = +0.93$~nats at 1.7B ($n=2$ seeds, both at
$+0.91/+0.94$).  Chain-shuffle confound
$\dsh = 0.000 \pm 0.010$ across all four 0.6B seeds and both 1.7B
seeds; evidence-lift $\elift = +0.001 \pm 0.006$, statistically
zero.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}  $\dsh \approx 0$ rules out a
non-chain-specific cause; the LM-on-LM-off gain is genuinely
conditioned on the chain.  $\elift \approx 0$ on a frozen-backbone
recipe is unusual: it says the writer is not encoding the literal
evidence session into $\Mc$.  The held-out TTT-readout audit
(\S\ref{sec:methodology} item~5, Appendix~\ref{app:ttt_readout})
confirms this directly: across all four 0.6B and three 1.7B F3-OFF
checkpoints, in all three TTT modes, the test-time-trained readout
\emph{cannot} recover the answer from $\Mc$ --- mean lift over the
floor is between $-0.66$ and $-1.07$~nats depending on mode and
scale, where the verdict threshold is $+0.30$.  The writer never
encoded the literal answer.  $\Mc$ is therefore best understood as
encoding chain-\emph{conditional context} --- style, topic,
vocabulary, prior turns --- that shifts the LM toward better priors
on continuations of the chain, rather than as encoding literal
evidence for retrieval.  This is a different mechanism from the
``memory recall'' the joint-training and retrieval programs target,
and it is consistent with the modest $\dnm$ we observe relative to
oracle-RAG: the surviving recipe is a chain-context conditioner, not
a fact memorizer.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We separate the limitations of this paper into three axes: scope,
architectural class, and methodological coverage.

\paragraph{Scope.}  Our results are reported on Qwen3-0.6B and
Qwen3-1.7B and on a single architectural family (slot-attention
writer, depth-routed cross-attention readout).  The F3-OFF flip is
established at $n=4$ seeds at 0.6B and $n=2$ seeds at 1.7B;
behaviour at scale ($\ge 7$B) and on non-slot writer architectures
(NTM-style external memory, Perceiver-IO-style fixed-bandwidth
latents) is open.  We test one corpus family for the
writer-objective failure (the random-code corpus) and report the
held-out $\Mc$-probe behaviour on the LongMemEval-S validation
split, $50$ chains; LoCoMo~\cite{locomo2024} and natural multi-week
dialogue corpora remain future work.

\paragraph{Architectural-level versus measurement-level claims.}
Four of the six failure modes (FM1, FM3, FM4, FM5) are
evaluation-level: they live in the metric, the corpus, or the floor
definition, and generalise to any recurrent-memory paper using the
affected metric or corpus regardless of architecture.  Two (FM2 and
FM6) are architecture-level.  We state the architectural class in
each case and exhibit the failure on Memory Residuals at
$\le 1.7$B; we do not claim, e.g., that all joint-trained
architectures collapse $\elift$ to zero in all training regimes,
only that this is the failure mode the joint-training program must
rule out and that a published-style architecture in the program has
been observed to exhibit it (FM2 example).

\paragraph{Sufficiency of the audit battery.}  We do not claim the
six-item audit battery is \emph{sufficient} to certify a
recurrent-memory result.  We claim it is \emph{necessary}: each of
the six controls catches a failure that is independently load-bearing
on at least one ideology, and the union catches all failures we have
observed in the campaign.  New corpora and new architectures may
surface new failure modes the battery does not capture.

\paragraph{The F3-OFF surprise.}  We do not claim auxiliary
readout-side probe losses are universally harmful.  We tested one
writer family (slot-attention) at $\le 1.7$B with a chain-identification
probe target.  The effect at scale, with a different probe target
(e.g.\ callback-content prediction rather than chain ID), or in
non-slot writer architectures is open.  The
chain-conditional-context interpretation of $\elift \approx 0$ on
the surviving recipe is supported by the held-out TTT-readout audit
(Appendix~\ref{app:ttt_readout}); a positive result on a stronger
writer-side extractive objective would re-open the question.

\paragraph{Cross-architectural validation.}  The claim that the
field-level reporting matrix in Table~\ref{tab:ideologies}
systematically under-reports controls is supported by a literature
audit of $18$ representative papers
(Appendix~\ref{app:litaudit}); we do not claim it is exhaustive,
and individual papers may have addressed a control in a way the
audit misses.  The matrix is intended as a structured argument, not
as a final scoring of the field.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The conservative answer to ``when does recurrent memory help?'' is
``when no other path can solve the proxy''.  Across the campaign,
that condition factored into six independent failure modes that map
onto three ideological research programs.  The six-item audit
methodology (\S\ref{sec:methodology}) certifies a single recipe ---
frozen backbone, slot-attention $\Mc$, depth-routed readout,
$\amem$-floor, and \emph{no} auxiliary readout probe --- that
survives all six.  The F3-OFF empirical surprise refutes the
joint-training prescription that auxiliary writer/readout
supervision is needed for FM6: the LM-NLL signal is sufficient when
the architectural constraints (frozen backbone, $\amem$-floor,
readout depth) are in place.  The TTT-readout audit on the surviving
recipe (Appendix~\ref{app:ttt_readout}) further refines the
interpretation: $\Mc$ encodes chain-conditional context, not literal
evidence.  Future work the methodology points to includes scaling
the surviving recipe past $1.7$B, testing the F3-OFF effect under
non-slot writer families, and extending the audit battery to natural
long-term memory corpora such as LoCoMo~\cite{locomo2024}.

\section{Architecture and Training Details}
\label{app:arch}

\paragraph{Architectural hyperparameters.}  Memory size $K=128$ slots
at backbone hidden width $d$; extract source layer
\texttt{hidden\_14} with input RMSNorm; slot-attention judge with
$3$ iterations, orthogonal query initialisation, slot positional
embeddings, and judge QK layer-norm; iterative readout depth
$L_R=4$; depth-routed Block-Attention-Residual readout with learned
$\amem$-floor.  Trainable parameter count $41.5$M at Qwen3-0.6B
($\sim\!6\%$ overhead) and $164.8$M at Qwen3-1.7B ($\sim\!8\%$).

\paragraph{Training hyperparameters.}  Optimiser AdamW with
$\beta_1{=}0.9$, $\beta_2{=}0.95$, weight decay $0.1$; cosine LR
schedule with $200$ warmup steps and $5\%$ minimum ratio; learning
rates $\eta_{\Mc} = 3{\times}10^{-4}$ for memory-side parameters and
$\eta_{\mathrm{LM}} = 0$ (frozen).  Batch size $4$ chains with
gradient accumulation $4$ (effective $16$); $1000$ optimiser steps;
bf16 precision with gradient checkpointing.

\paragraph{Compute.}  Each 0.6B run uses approximately $1.5$
H100-hours on a single H100 80GB; each 1.7B run approximately
$6$~H100-hours.  Total campaign compute, including unreported
preliminary runs from earlier ablations, is approximately $400$
H100-hours.

\section{TTT-Readout Audit on the Surviving Recipe}
\label{app:ttt_readout}

The TTT-readout audit (item~5 of the methodology,
\S\ref{sec:methodology}) is run on the four F3-OFF v27b 0.6B
checkpoints and the three F3-OFF v28 1.7B checkpoints via
\texttt{Scripts/run\_ttt\_mc\_readout\_v27b\_v28.sh}.  Three TTT
modes are tested per checkpoint: \texttt{v\_only} (TTT on $\Mc$ and
the readout's $W_V$ only), \texttt{qkv} (TTT on $\Mc$ and all three
readout projections $W_Q,W_K,W_V$), and \texttt{qkv\_reset} (as
\texttt{qkv} but the readout $W_Q,W_K,W_V$ are re-initialised from
$\mathcal{N}(0,d^{-1/2})$ before TTT, testing intrinsic readout
capacity from a clean init).  Each cell uses $K=80$ Adam steps with
$\eta_{\Mc}=10^{-2}$, $\eta_{\mathrm{readout}}=10^{-3}$, $n=16$
held-out chains.  The per-chain decision metric is
$\mathrm{lift}_{\mathrm{floor}} = \CEfloor - \mathrm{CE}_{\mathrm{ttt}}$;
$> +0.30$~nats indicates the writer encoded the answer and the
readout was the bottleneck; $\le 0$ indicates the writer did not
encode the literal answer.

\paragraph{Result.}  Across all $7$ checkpoints and all $3$ modes
($21$ cells, $336$ chain-level evaluations) the audit returns the
\textbf{negative} verdict (Table~\ref{tab:ttt_readout}): mean
lift-vs-floor between $-0.66$ and $-1.07$~nats depending on mode
and scale, well below the $+0.30$~nat threshold for ``readout
bottleneck''.  Even the most aggressive setting,
\texttt{qkv\_reset}, which re-initialises the readout from scratch
and TTT-trains all three projection weights jointly with $\Mc$ on
the evidence-session loss, cannot recover the answer from $\Mc$.
The writer never encoded the literal answer; $\Mc$ encodes
chain-conditional context.  The 1.7B checkpoints show \emph{larger}
negative lifts than 0.6B, consistent with the surviving recipe
encoding a more diffuse chain-context representation at scale.

\section{Literature Audit Notes}
\label{app:litaudit}

This appendix supports Table~\ref{tab:ideologies}'s cell-level
judgments.  We summarise, per paper, what controls are reported and
which of our six diagnostics are addressed; full derivations are in
the campaign's audit memo \texttt{audit\_b\_literature.md}.

\paragraph{Joint-training cluster.}
\emph{Memorising Transformers}~\cite{memorizing2022} reports
natural-text LM perplexity with a memory-size ablation; no
callback-aware CE, no evidence-redacted floor, no closed-book or
template-prior baseline.  \emph{AutoCompressors}~\cite{autocompressors2023}
reports memory-on/off ablations (a partial floor) but no
template-prior audit and no callback-token-only CE.
\emph{TRIME}~\cite{trime2022} uses contextualised in-batch
positives/negatives and matched training/testing memory conditions;
the matched conditions are a weak prior control.
\emph{RIMs}~\cite{rims2021} and the NTM/DNC family~\cite{ntm2014}
use copy/reverse/associative-recall algorithmic tasks; the
algorithmic synthesis controls task identity but not category-cue
priors.

\paragraph{Frozen-backbone cluster.}
\emph{RMT}~\cite{rmt2022} reports CE on
copy/reverse/associative-retrieval and on quadratic equations; no
callback-aware CE or template-prior baseline.
\emph{ARMT}~\cite{armt2024} extends RMT to associative recall with
very long contexts; controls are similar.
\emph{Block-Recurrent Transformer}~\cite{blockrecurrent2022} reports
PG19/arXiv/GitHub perplexity against Transformer-XL baselines; no
callback-style controls.  \emph{Compressive
Transformer}~\cite{compressive2019} reports natural-document
perplexity; no callback-style controls.

\paragraph{Retrieval cluster.}
\emph{RAG}~\cite{ragoriginal2020} reports task accuracy (ODQA, fact
verification); window leakage is implicit in the design but not
measured as a leak.  \emph{DOS-RAG}~\cite{ragbaseline2025} is
explicitly a stronger-baselines paper; reports a closed-book
baseline and discusses retrieval position effects, partial coverage
of FM3 and FM4.

\paragraph{Long-context-evaluation cluster.}
\emph{NoLiMa}~\cite{nolima2025} explicitly removes literal overlap
between query and evidence (the closest analogue to our FM3) and
demonstrates that scores drop sharply when the overlap is removed;
counts as $\checkmark$ on Prior and on the FM3 sense of WLk.
\emph{RULER}~\cite{ruler2024} adds UUIDs and hard distractor
needles, addressing FM3 (closed-set leak) and FM4 (window leakage as
a function of haystack design); also $\checkmark$ on Prior and WLk.
\emph{Lost-in-the-Middle}~\cite{lostmiddle2024} documents position
effects with random-UUID K/V retrieval as a control; partial credit
on Prior.  \emph{LongBench}~\cite{longbench2024} reports a
closed-book/no-context baseline as a primary control; $\checkmark$
on Prior; partial credit on CB-CE because multi-task accuracy can be
sensitive to per-task answer-token distribution.
\emph{BABILong}~\cite{babilong2024} embeds bAbI facts in PG19
distractors; partial control on Prior and WLk.
\emph{ZeroSCROLLS}~\cite{zeroscrolls2023} contributes
contamination-controlled zero-shot evaluation; partial Prior.
\emph{LongMemEval}~\cite{longmemeval2025} explicitly separates
indexing, retrieval, and reading; the closest existing analogue to
our TTT-readout audit (item~5), counted as $\sim$ rather than
$\checkmark$ because the separation is structural (different stages)
rather than an interventional readout-only fine-tune.

\paragraph{Aggregate.}  Of $18$ papers audited, $0$ report all six
diagnostics; the maximum is two diagnostics with quantitative
evidence (NoLiMa, RULER), and the modal count is zero.  The cluster
that reports the most controls is the long-context-evaluation
cluster, which has imported anti-shortcut techniques but not
exported them back into recurrent-memory architecture evaluation.
